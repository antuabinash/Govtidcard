4. Long Summary (English) "Road Safety Week" is a highly informative text that underscores the paramount importance of road safety to prevent injuries and save lives. Every year, countless lives are lost due to a lack of awareness about traffic rules, as reported by the World Health Organization. To combat this, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways in India organizes the 'Road Safety Week' campaign every January. This national event, supported by NGOs and local authorities, aims to sensitize the public against dangerous habits like drunken driving, high-speed driving, and driving without helmets or seatbelts. During this week, massive awareness drives including poster distributions, medical check-ups, school competitions, and strict police checking are conducted across major cities.

The chapter details five primary factors responsible for fatal road accidents. First is drunken and rash driving, which risks multiple lives. Second is underage driving; the legal age for a private vehicle is 18, and 20 for commercial ones, though 16-year-olds can drive 50cc non-gear vehicles with parental consent. Third is distracted driving, specifically talking or texting on mobile phones or listening to loud music while behind the wheel. Fourth is the violation of basic rules like jumping traffic signals or not wearing seatbelts. Lastly, the presence of poorly maintained commercial vehicles and careless pedestrians heavily contributes to the casualty rate.

To prevent these accidents, the text suggests strict enforcement of laws. Authorities must heavily fine those caught driving drunk, without helmets, or using phones. The process of issuing driving licenses should be made stringent, and fitness certificates for all running vehicles must be made compulsory. Ultimately, Road Safety Week serves as a crucial reminder of the preciousness of human life, urging everyone to take responsibility for their own safety and the safety of others on the road.

Question 2: What is the main finding of the World Health Organisation regarding road accidents? (ସଡ଼କ ଦୁର୍ଘଟଣା ସମ୍ପର୍କରେ ବିଶ୍ୱ ସ୍ୱାସ୍ଥ୍ୟ ସଂଗଠନର ମୁଖ୍ୟ ଅନୁସନ୍ଧାନ କ'ଣ?)

Answer: According to the World Health Organisation, the majority of hospitalisation cases and the leading causes of death on the roads are due to a lack of awareness about road safety rules among people.

Question 3: When is the Road Safety Week organized and by which ministry? (ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସପ୍ତାହ କେବେ ଏବଂ କେଉଁ ମନ୍ତ୍ରଣାଳୟ ଦ୍ୱାରା ଆୟୋଜିତ ହୁଏ?)

Answer: The Road Safety Week is organized every year in the month of January. It is organized by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways of India.

Question 4: Why is there a growing need for road safety awareness today? (ଆଜିକାଲି ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସଚେତନତାର ଅଧିକ ଆବଶ୍ୟକତା କାହିଁକି ରହିଛି?)

Answer: There is a growing need for awareness because there is a continuous rise in the number of road accidents. This rise is primarily due to the rapid increase in the number of vehicle users on the roads.

Question 5: What is the primary aim of the Road Safety Week campaign? (ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସପ୍ତାହ ଅଭିଯାନର ପ୍ରାଥମିକ ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ କ'ଣ?)

Answer: The primary aim is to raise public awareness about traffic rules, reduce road accidents, and sensitise people about the dangers of drunken driving, high-speed driving, and the importance of using helmets and seat-belts.

Question 6: What are the age criteria for getting different types of driving licenses in India? (ଭାରତରେ ବିଭିନ୍ନ ପ୍ରକାରର ଡ୍ରାଇଭିଂ ଲାଇସେନ୍ସ ପାଇବା ପାଇଁ ବୟସ ସୀମା କ'ଣ?)

Answer: In India, the permissible age for driving a private vehicle is 18 years. A 16-year-old can drive a 50cc gearless vehicle with parental consent, and one must be 20 years old to drive a commercial vehicle.

Question 7: How do commercial vehicles contribute to road accidents? (ବ୍ୟବସାୟିକ ଯାନଗୁଡ଼ିକ ସଡ଼କ ଦୁର୍ଘଟଣାରେ କିପରି ସହାୟକ ହୁଅନ୍ତି?)

Answer: Many commercial vehicles that run on the roads are unsafe and poorly maintained. These badly kept vehicles frequently break down or lose control, risking the lives of the drivers as well as other people on the road.

Question 8: What is considered "distracted driving" according to the text? (ପାଠ୍ୟ ଅନୁଯାୟୀ "ଅନ୍ୟମନସ୍କ ଚାଳନା" କାହାକୁ କୁହାଯାଏ?)

Answer: Distracted driving includes activities that take the driver's attention away from the road, such as speaking on cell phones, texting while driving, talking to other passengers, and listening to loud music.

Question 9: What specific activities are organized for drivers during Road Safety Week? (ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସପ୍ତାହରେ ଡ୍ରାଇଭରମାନଙ୍କ ପାଇଁ କେଉଁ ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦିଷ୍ଟ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟକ୍ରମ ଆୟୋଜନ କରାଯାଏ?)

Answer: During Road Safety Week, authorities and organizations arrange free medical check-up camps and special driving training workshops specifically aimed at educating and testing the drivers.

Question 10: How do careless pedestrians put themselves and others in trouble? (ଅସାବଧାନ ପଥଚାରୀମାନେ ନିଜକୁ ଏବଂ ଅନ୍ୟମାନଙ୍କୁ କିପରି ଅସୁବିଧାରେ ପକାନ୍ତି?)

Answer: Careless pedestrians who do not follow traffic signals, cross roads without looking, or walk on the wrong side of the road often cause sudden disruptions in traffic flow, leading to fatal accidents.

Question 11: What does the term "Zero Tolerance against Drunken Driving" mean in the context of the chapter? (ଅଧ୍ୟାୟ ସନ୍ଦର୍ଭରେ "ମଦ୍ୟପାନ କରି ଗାଡ଼ି ଚଳାଇବା ବିରୁଦ୍ଧରେ ଶୂନ୍ୟ ସହନଶୀଳତା" ର ଅର୍ଥ କ'ଣ?)

Answer: It means that local authorities should conduct thorough checks and show no mercy to anyone caught driving under the influence of alcohol. They must be heavily fined or punished without any exception.

Question 12: Why should the fitness certificate be made compulsory for vehicles? (ଯାନବାହାନଗୁଡ଼ିକ ପାଇଁ ଫିଟନେସ୍ ପ୍ରମାଣପତ୍ର ବାଧ୍ୟତାମୂଳକ କାହିଁକି ହେବା ଉଚିତ୍?)

Answer: A compulsory fitness certificate ensures that only safe, well-maintained, and road-worthy vehicles are allowed to run. This prevents accidents caused by mechanical failures in old and poorly kept vehicles.

Question 13: Mention any two careless habits of drivers that cause casualties. (ଚାଳକମାନଙ୍କର ଯେକୌଣସି ଦୁଇଟି ଅସାବଧାନ ଅଭ୍ୟାସ ଉଲ୍ଲେଖ କର ଯାହା ମୃତାହତର କାରଣ ହୁଏ।)

Answer: Two highly careless habits of drivers that lead to severe casualties are jumping traffic signals instead of waiting, and driving in a restricted lane or the wrong side of the road.

Question 14: What advice regarding mobile phones is given to drivers during the campaign? (ଅଭିଯାନ ସମୟରେ ମୋବାଇଲ୍ ଫୋନ୍ ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧରେ ଡ୍ରାଇଭରମାନଙ୍କୁ କେଉଁ ପରାମର୍ଶ ଦିଆଯାଏ?)

Answer: Drivers are strictly instructed not to use their cell phones for talking or texting, and not to listen to loud music while driving, as it distracts them and leads to serious mishaps.

Question 15: Ultimately, what is the core message of the Road Safety Week? (ପରିଶେଷରେ, ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସପ୍ତାହର ମୂଳ ବାର୍ତ୍ତା କ'ଣ?)

Answer: The core message is to make people recognize the preciousness of human life. It teaches individuals what specific steps must be taken to save their own lives and the lives of others on the road.

Question 16: Discuss the five major factors that cause serious road accidents as highlighted in the text. (ପାଠ୍ୟରେ ଦର୍ଶାଯାଇଥିବା ସାଂଘାତିକ ସଡ଼କ ଦୁର୍ଘଟଣାର ପାଞ୍ଚଟି ପ୍ରମୁଖ କାରଣ ଆଲୋଚନା କର।)

Answer: According to the text, five major factors cause fatal road accidents. Firstly, drunken and rash driving, often accompanied by over-speeding, risks many lives. Secondly, underage driving by unqualified individuals below the permissible age limit leads to crashes. Thirdly, distracted driving, which includes texting, talking on cell phones, or listening to loud music, breaks the driver's focus. Fourthly, careless habits like jumping traffic signals, driving in restricted lanes, and not wearing seat belts or helmets increase fatalities. Finally, the presence of careless pedestrians and poorly maintained, unsafe commercial vehicles on the road significantly contribute to serious mishaps.

Question 17: Describe the various activities and programs organized during the Road Safety Week Campaign. (ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସପ୍ତାହ ଅଭିଯାନ ସମୟରେ ଆୟୋଜିତ ବିଭିନ୍ନ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟକଳାପ ଏବଂ ପ୍ରୋଗ୍ରାମର ବର୍ଣ୍ଣନା କର।)

Answer: During Road Safety Week in January, major cities witness a variety of awareness activities. Educational banners, safety posters, and leaflets are widely distributed to on-road travelers. Schools and colleges participate voluntarily by organizing painting and drawing competitions, debates, and tests on road rules. For drivers, free medical check-up camps and driving training workshops are held. Local authorities and police conduct thorough checks to enforce "Zero Tolerance against Drunken Driving" and explain traffic rules and cautions to regular commuters. The collective effort involves the government, NGOs, and private firms working together.

Question 18: What strict preventive measures does the author suggest to avoid road accidents? (ସଡ଼କ ଦୁର୍ଘଟଣାକୁ ଏଡ଼ାଇବା ପାଇଁ ଲେଖକ କେଉଁ କଠୋର ପ୍ରତିଷେଧକ ମୂଳକ ପଦକ୍ଷେପ ପରାମର୍ଶ ଦେଇଛନ୍ତି?)

Answer: To prevent accidents, the author suggests strict enforcement of rules by local authorities. They must actively check all drivers and motorcyclists for drunken driving. The government must issue strict orders to license-issuing authorities to take strong steps and ensure only qualified people get licenses. Persons caught talking or texting on phones while driving must be heavily fined or punished. Fines must also be strictly imposed on those not wearing helmets or seat-belts. Furthermore, fitness certificates must be made absolutely compulsory for every vehicle running on the road, with zero tolerance for safety violations.

Question 19: Explain the concept of "Zero Tolerance against Drunken Driving". Why is it crucial? ("ମଦ୍ୟପାନ କରି ଗାଡ଼ି ଚଳାଇବା ବିରୁଦ୍ଧରେ ଶୂନ୍ୟ ସହନଶୀଳତା" ର ଧାରଣା ବୁଝାଅ। ଏହା କାହିଁକି ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ?)

Answer: The concept of "Zero Tolerance against Drunken Driving" means that law enforcement authorities will show absolutely no leniency or patience towards anyone caught driving under the influence of alcohol. It is extremely crucial because a drunken driver loses cognitive control, judgment, and reaction time, leading to rash, negligent, and over-speeding behaviors. Such a driver becomes a lethal threat, risking not only their own life but also the lives of innocent pedestrians and other commuters. Strict penalties (like a maximum fine of 2000 rupees) serve as a strong deterrent to stop this highly dangerous practice.

Question 20: Justify the statement: "Road Safety Week is an opportunity for us to recognise the preciousness of life". (ଏହି ଉକ୍ତିର ଯଥାର୍ଥତା ପ୍ରତିପାଦନ କର: "ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ସପ୍ତାହ ହେଉଛି ଜୀବନର ମୂଲ୍ୟବାନତାକୁ ଚିହ୍ନିବା ପାଇଁ ଆମ ପାଇଁ ଏକ ସୁଯୋଗ"।)

Answer: The statement beautifully captures the essence of the campaign. In our fast-paced daily lives, people often ignore simple rules like wearing a helmet or avoiding phone calls while driving, treating safety as non-essential. Road Safety Week forces us to pause and look at the grim statistics of injuries and deaths caused entirely by human error and negligence. By spreading awareness and educating the masses about safe practices, the week acts as a reminder that human life is fragile and irreplaceable. It teaches us that by valuing our own life and respecting the lives of others, we can prevent tragic, unnecessary deaths.

Question 21: How do "distracted driving" and "underage driving" threaten road safety? ("ଅନ୍ୟମନସ୍କ ଚାଳନା" ଏବଂ "କମ୍ ବୟସରେ ଗାଡ଼ି ଚଳାଇବା" ସଡ଼କ ସୁରକ୍ଷା ପ୍ରତି କିପରି ବିପଦ ସୃଷ୍ଟି କରେ?)

Answer: "Distracted driving" occurs when a driver's attention is diverted from the road due to talking or texting on a mobile phone, chatting with passengers, or listening to loud music. This delayed reaction time often leads to deadly collisions. "Underage driving" is equally dangerous. Children below the permissible age (18 for private vehicles, 20 for commercial) lack the necessary training, maturity, and legal qualification to handle a vehicle in complex traffic situations. When unqualified, underage drivers take the wheel, they lack the experience to prevent accidents, thereby posing a severe threat to themselves and the general public.

Question 22: Based on the 'Road Offences and Penalties' chart, discuss how financial penalties act as a deterrent against traffic rule violations. ('ସଡ଼କ ନିୟମ ଉଲ୍ଲଂଘନ ଏବଂ ଜୋରିମାନା' ଚାର୍ଟ ଉପରେ ଆଧାର କରି ଆଲୋଚନା କର ଯେ ଆର୍ଥିକ ଜୋରିମାନା କିପରି ଟ୍ରାଫିକ୍ ନିୟମ ଉଲ୍ଲଂଘନ ବିରୁଦ୍ଧରେ ଏକ ପ୍ରତିବନ୍ଧକ ଭାବରେ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟ କରେ।)

Answer: Financial penalties play a significant role in enforcing discipline on the roads. According to the chart, serious offences attract heavy fines, such as Rs. 2000 for drunken driving, Rs. 1000 or imprisonment for dangerous driving, and Rs. 500 for underage driving or driving without a license. Even common violations like not wearing a helmet or parking on footpaths attract a fine of Rs. 100. When commuters know that careless behavior will directly impact their finances or lead to imprisonment, they are more likely to obey the rules. These penalties act as a strong psychological deterrent, forcing citizens to adopt safer driving habits out of fear of the law.

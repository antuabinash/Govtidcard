✍️ Section 9: Long Answer (3–4 Marks) ✍️

Answer 106: 🌍 Neem Baba originated millions of years ago in the regions of North India or Myanmar. Because of its incredible adaptability and usefulness, the tree species soon spread beyond India's borders. Today, the neem family can be found thriving in numerous Asian and African countries, and even in some parts of America.

Answer 107: 📜 The various names highlight the tree's universal value. The Sanskrit name 'Arishta' reflects its medicinal power to cure diseases. The fact that Iranians named it 'Neem' shows its international trade history. Scientists call it 'nature's gift to man' and 'bitter grace of God' out of sheer respect for its endless organic, health, and environmental benefits.

Answer 108: 🌿 Every part of the tree is a pharmacy. Neem leaves destroy germs and soothe measles itching. The bark was used by Amber's grandmother to cure a severe respiratory cough. Furthermore, the delicate neem flowers were utilized to create a soothing eye kajal to cure her father's itchy eyes, proving the tree's versatility.

Answer 109: ⚠️ Neem Baba warned her because natural medicines contain potent chemical properties. If the wrong part of the tree is used, or the dosage is incorrect, it could harm the patient instead of curing them. Therefore, remedies must only be prepared by individuals who are well-trained and have vast experience in traditional medicine.

Answer 110: 🧑‍🌾 Neem acts as a safe, natural pesticide. When seed powder is mixed with water and sprayed, it stops locusts from eating plant leaves. In rice fields, it prevents mosquitoes from breeding in standing water, thereby increasing crop yield. It also protects the roots of the crops from being devoured by underground termites.

Answer 111: 🦟 The text highlights a very simple and cost-effective method: using neem seed powder. In agriculture, throwing this powder into the standing water of rice fields stops mosquitoes from multiplying. Similarly, households can apply this exact same powder to any stagnant water around their homes, neutralizing mosquito threats safely and cheaply.

Answer 112: 🏡 Beyond medicine, neem is essential for daily living. Neem twigs are used as toothbrushes. Leaves are kept in cupboards to protect clothes. Seed powder is mixed with food grains for safe storage. Neem oil is extracted to manufacture soaps and toothpaste, and its wood is used to build highly durable, termite-proof furniture.

Fables and Folk Tales- A Bottle of Dew

Fables and Folk Tales- A Bottle of Dew – Study Material Class 6 Jasmine English

👩‍🏫 About the Author: Sudha Murty

✍️ Biography: Sudha Murty is a highly respected Indian educator, author, and philanthropist. Born in 1950 in Shiggaon, Karnataka, she began her career as an engineer and later became the chairperson of the Infosys Foundation. She is widely celebrated for her extensive social work and her remarkable contributions to Indian literature in both English and Kannada.

📚 Famous Works: Her most popular books include "How I Taught My Grandmother to Read", "Wise and Otherwise", "Grandma's Bag of Stories", "The Magic Drum and Other Favourite Stories", and "Dollar Bahu".

🖋️ Writing Style: Her writing style is known for its absolute simplicity, clarity, and warmth. She writes in a conversational tone that connects easily with young readers. Her stories always carry profound moral lessons, focusing on human values, hard work, honesty, and everyday realities.

🌿 Each and Every Paragraph Explanation

➡️ Paragraph 1

📜 Original Paragraph: Rama Natha was the son of a rich landlord. His father left him large areas of land when he died. But Rama Natha did not spend even one day looking after his land. This was because he had a funny idea. He believed there was a magic potion that could turn any object into gold. He spent all his time learning more about this magic potion. People cheated him often, promising to tell him about it, but he did not give up. His wife, Madhumati, was tired of this. She was also worried because she saw how much money Rama Natha was spending. She was sure that soon they would be without money.

🗣️ English-Odia (Pronunciation): ରାମା ନାଥା ୱାଜ୍ ଦ ସନ୍ ଅଫ୍ ଏ ରିଚ୍ ଲାଣ୍ଡଲର୍ଡ। ହିଜ୍ ଫାଦର୍ ଲେଫ୍ଟ ହିମ୍ ଲାର୍ଜ ଏରିଆଜ୍ ଅଫ୍ ଲାଣ୍ଡ୍ ହ୍ୱେନ୍ ହି ଡାଏଡ୍। ବଟ୍ ରାମା ନାଥା ଡିଡ୍ ନଟ୍ ସ୍ପେଣ୍ଡ୍ ଇଭେନ୍ ୱାନ୍ ଡେ ଲୁକିଙ୍ଗ୍ ଆଫ୍ଟର୍ ହିଜ୍ ଲାଣ୍ଡ୍। ଦିସ୍ ୱାଜ୍ ବିକଜ୍ ହି ହ୍ୟାଡ୍ ଏ ଫନି ଆଇଡିଆ। ହି ବିଲିଭ୍‌ଡ୍ ଦେଆର୍ ୱାଜ୍ ଏ ମ୍ୟାଜିକ୍ ପୋସନ୍ ଦ୍ୟାଟ୍ କୁଡ୍ ଟର୍ଣ୍ଣ ଏନି ଅବଜେକ୍ଟ୍ ଇନଟୁ ଗୋଲ୍ଡ। ହି ସ୍ପେଣ୍ଟ୍ ଅଲ୍ ହିଜ୍ ଟାଇମ୍ ଲର୍ଣ୍ଣିଂ ମୋର୍ ଆବାଉଟ୍ ଦିସ୍ ମ୍ୟାଜିକ୍ ପୋସନ୍। ପିପୁଲ୍ ଚିଟେଡ୍ ହିମ୍ ଅଫନ୍, ପ୍ରମିସିଂ ଟୁ ଟେଲ୍ ହିମ୍ ଆବାଉଟ୍ ଇଟ୍, ବଟ୍ ହି ଡିଡ୍ ନଟ୍ ଗିଭ୍ ଅପ୍। ହିଜ୍ ୱାଇଫ୍, ମଧୁମତୀ, ୱାଜ୍ ଟାୟାର୍ଡ ଅଫ୍ ଦିସ୍। ସି ୱାଜ୍ ଅଲସୋ ଓରିଡ୍ ବିକଜ୍ ସି ସ ହାଓ ମଚ୍ ମନି ରାମା ନାଥା ୱାଜ୍ ସ୍ପେଣ୍ଡିଂ। ସି ୱାଜ୍ ସିଓର୍ ଦ୍ୟାଟ୍ ସୁନ୍ ଦେ ଉଡ୍ ବି ୱିଦାଉଟ୍ ମନି।

📝 Odia Meaning (All Sentences): ରମାନାଥ ଜଣେ ଧନୀ ଜମିଦାରଙ୍କ ପୁଅ ଥିଲେ। ତାଙ୍କ ପିତାଙ୍କ ମୃତ୍ୟୁ ପରେ ସେ ତାଙ୍କ ପାଇଁ ବହୁତ ଜମିବାଡ଼ି ଛାଡ଼ି ଯାଇଥିଲେ। କିନ୍ତୁ ରମାନାଥ ଗୋଟିଏ ଦିନ ବି ନିଜ ଜମିର ଯତ୍ନ ନେବାରେ ବିତାଇ ନଥିଲେ। ଏହାର କାରଣ ଥିଲା ଯେ ତାଙ୍କର ଗୋଟିଏ ହାସ୍ୟାସ୍ପଦ ଧାରଣା ଥିଲା। ସେ ବିଶ୍ୱାସ କରୁଥିଲେ ଯେ ଏକ ଯାଦୁକରୀ ପାଣି ଅଛି ଯାହାକି ଯେକୌଣସି ଜିନିଷକୁ ସୁନାରେ ପରିଣତ କରିପାରିବ। ସେ ଏହି ଯାଦୁକରୀ ପାଣି ବିଷୟରେ ଅଧିକ ଜାଣିବା ପାଇଁ ନିଜର ସମସ୍ତ ସମୟ ନଷ୍ଟ କରୁଥିଲେ। ଲୋକମାନେ ତାଙ୍କୁ ବାରମ୍ବାର ଏହା ବିଷୟରେ କହିବାର ପ୍ରତିଶ୍ରୁତି ଦେଇ ଠକି ଦେଉଥିଲେ, କିନ୍ତୁ ସେ ହାର ମାନୁନଥିଲେ। ତାଙ୍କ ପତ୍ନୀ, ମଧୁମତୀ, ଏସବୁ ଦେଖି ବିରକ୍ତ ହୋଇଯାଇଥିଲେ। ସେ ମଧ୍ୟ ଚିନ୍ତିତ ଥିଲେ କାରଣ ରମାନାଥ କେତେ ଟଙ୍କା ଖର୍ଚ୍ଚ କରୁଛନ୍ତି ତାହା ସେ ଦେଖୁଥିଲେ। ସେ ନିଶ୍ଚିତ ଥିଲେ ଯେ ଖୁବ୍ ଶୀଘ୍ର ସେମାନଙ୍କ ପାଖରେ ଆଉ ଟଙ୍କା ନଥିବ।

💡 English Meaning: Rama Natha inherited a lot of land from his rich father, but he ignored it. Instead, he wasted his time and money searching for a magical liquid that could change things into gold. People constantly tricked him, which made his wife Madhumati very worried that they would soon become poor.

➡️ Paragraph 2

📜 Original Paragraph: One day, a famous sage called Mahipati came to their town. Rama Natha became his follower and asked him about the potion. To his surprise the sage answered, "Yes, in my travels in the Himalayas, I heard how you could make such a potion. But it is difficult."

🗣️ English-Odia (Pronunciation): ୱାନ୍ ଡେ, ଏ ଫେମସ୍ ସେଜ୍ କଲଡ୍ ମହୀପତି କେମ୍ ଟୁ ଦେଆର୍ ଟାଉନ୍। ରାମା ନାଥା ବିକେମ୍ ହିଜ୍ ଫଲୋଅର୍ ଆଣ୍ଡ୍ ଆସ୍କଡ୍ ହିମ୍ ଆବାଉଟ୍ ଦ ପୋସନ୍। ଟୁ ହିଜ୍ ସରପ୍ରାଇଜ୍ ଦ ସେଜ୍ ଆନ୍ସର୍ଡ, "ୟେସ୍, ଇନ୍ ମାଇ ଟ୍ରାଭେଲ୍ସ ଇନ୍ ଦ ହିମାଲୟାଜ୍, ଆଇ ହାର୍ଡ ହାଓ ୟୁ କୁଡ୍ ମେକ୍ ସଚ୍ ଏ ପୋସନ୍। ବଟ୍ ଇଟ୍ ଇଜ୍ ଡିଫିକଲ୍ଟ।"

📝 Odia Meaning (All Sentences): ଦିନେ, ମହୀପତି ନାମକ ଜଣେ ପ୍ରସିଦ୍ଧ ସାଧୁ ସେମାନଙ୍କ ସହରକୁ ଆସିଲେ। ରମାନାଥ ତାଙ୍କର ଅନୁଗାମୀ ହୋଇଗଲେ ଏବଂ ତାଙ୍କୁ ସେହି ଯାଦୁକରୀ ପାଣି ବିଷୟରେ ପଚାରିଲେ। ତାଙ୍କୁ ଆଶ୍ଚର୍ଯ୍ୟ କରି ସାଧୁ ଉତ୍ତର ଦେଲେ, "ହଁ, ହିମାଳୟରେ ମୋର ଯାତ୍ରା ସମୟରେ, ମୁଁ ଶୁଣିଥିଲି ଯେ ତୁମେ କିପରି ସେହି ଯାଦୁକରୀ ପାଣି ତିଆରି କରିପାରିବ। କିନ୍ତୁ ଏହା ବହୁତ କଷ୍ଟକର।"

💡 English Meaning: A well-known holy man named Mahipati visited their area. Rama Natha followed him and asked about the gold-making liquid. The sage surprised him by saying that he knew how to make it, but the process was very hard.

➡️ Paragraph 3

📜 Original Paragraph: "Tell me!" requested Rama Natha. "You have to plant a banana plant and water it regularly with your hands. In winter, the morning dew is on the leaves. You have to collect the dew and store it in a bottle. When you have five litres of dew, bring it to me. I will chant some magic words, which will turn it into the magic potion. A drop of the potion will change any object into gold."

🗣️ English-Odia (Pronunciation): "ଟେଲ୍ ମି!" ରିକ୍ୱେଷ୍ଟେଡ୍ ରାମା ନାଥା। "ୟୁ ହ୍ୟାଭ୍ ଟୁ ପ୍ଲାଣ୍ଟ ଏ ବାନାନା ପ୍ଲାଣ୍ଟ ଆଣ୍ଡ୍ ୱାଟର୍ ଇଟ୍ ରେଗୁଲାର୍ଲି ୱିଥ୍ ୟୋର୍ ହ୍ୟାଣ୍ଡ୍ସ। ଇନ୍ ୱିଣ୍ଟର୍, ଦ ମର୍ଣ୍ଣିଂ ଡ୍ୟୁ ଇଜ୍ ଅନ୍ ଦ ଲିଭ୍‌ସ୍। ୟୁ ହ୍ୟାଭ୍ ଟୁ କଲେକ୍ଟ ଦ ଡ୍ୟୁ ଆଣ୍ଡ୍ ଷ୍ଟୋର୍ ଇଟ୍ ଇନ୍ ଏ ବୋଟଲ୍। ହ୍ୱେନ୍ ୟୁ ହ୍ୟାଭ୍ ଫାଇଭ୍ ଲିଟର୍ସ ଅଫ୍ ଡ୍ୟୁ, ବ୍ରିଙ୍ଗ ଇଟ୍ ଟୁ ମି। ଆଇ ୱିଲ୍ ଚାଣ୍ଟ୍ ସମ୍ ମ୍ୟାଜିକ୍ ୱାର୍ଡସ୍, ହ୍ୱିଚ୍ ୱିଲ୍ ଟର୍ଣ୍ଣ ଇଟ୍ ଇନଟୁ ଦ ମ୍ୟାଜିକ୍ ପୋସନ୍। ଏ ଡ୍ରପ୍ ଅଫ୍ ଦ ପୋସନ୍ ୱିଲ୍ ଚେଞ୍ଜ୍ ଏନି ଅବଜେକ୍ଟ୍ ଇନଟୁ ଗୋଲ୍ଡ।"

📝 Odia Meaning (All Sentences): "ମୋତେ କୁହନ୍ତୁ!" ରମାନାଥ ଅନୁରୋଧ କଲେ। "ତୁମକୁ ଗୋଟିଏ କଦଳୀ ଗଛ ଲଗାଇବାକୁ ପଡ଼ିବ ଏବଂ ନିୟମିତ ନିଜ ହାତରେ ତାକୁ ପାଣି ଦେବାକୁ ପଡ଼ିବ। ଶୀତଦିନେ, ପତ୍ର ଉପରେ ସକାଳର କାକର ପଡ଼ିଥାଏ। ତୁମକୁ ସେହି କାକର ସଂଗ୍ରହ କରି ଏକ ବୋତଲରେ ରଖିବାକୁ ପଡ଼ିବ। ଯେତେବେଳେ ତୁମ ପାଖରେ ୫ ଲିଟର କାକର ହୋଇଯିବ, ତାହା ମୋ ପାଖକୁ ଆଣିବ। ମୁଁ କିଛି ଯାଦୁକରୀ ମନ୍ତ୍ର ପଢ଼ିବି, ଯାହା ତାକୁ ଯାଦୁକରୀ ପାଣିରେ ପରିଣତ କରିଦେବ। ସେହି ପାଣିର ଗୋଟିଏ ବୁନ୍ଦା ଯେକୌଣସି ଜିନିଷକୁ ସୁନାରେ ବଦଳାଇ ଦେବ।"

💡 English Meaning: The sage explained the difficult process: Rama Natha had to grow banana plants, water them himself, and collect the winter morning dew from the leaves. Once he gathered five liters, the sage would magically convert the dew into the gold-making liquid.

➡️ Paragraph 4

📝 Odia Meaning (All Sentences): ବର୍ତ୍ତମାନ ସାଧୁ ଆଶ୍ଚର୍ଯ୍ୟ ହୋଇଥିବା ରମାନାଥଙ୍କ ଆଡ଼କୁ ବୁଲିପଡ଼ି କହିଲେ, "ଦୁନିଆରେ ଏମିତି କୌଣସି ଯାଦୁକରୀ ପାଣି ନାହିଁ ଯାହା ଜିନିଷକୁ ସୁନା କରିଦେବ। ତୁମେ ତୁମ ଜମିରେ କଠିନ ପରିଶ୍ରମ କଲ ଏବଂ ଏହି ବଗିଚା ସୃଷ୍ଟି କଲ। ଯେତେବେଳେ ତୁମେ ଗଛଗୁଡ଼ିକର ଯତ୍ନ ନେଉଥିଲ, ତୁମ ପତ୍ନୀ ସେହି ଫଳଗୁଡ଼ିକୁ ବଜାରରେ ବିକ୍ରି କରୁଥିଲେ। ସେହିପରି ଭାବରେ ହିଁ ତୁମେ ଏହି ଟଙ୍କା ପାଇଛ। ତୁମର କଠିନ ପରିଶ୍ରମ ହିଁ ଏହି ଧନ ସୃଷ୍ଟି କରିଛି, କୌଣସି ଯାଦୁ ନୁହେଁ। ଯଦି ମୁଁ ତୁମକୁ ଆଗରୁ ଏ ବିଷୟରେ କହିଥାନ୍ତି, ତେବେ ତୁମେ ମୋ କଥା ଶୁଣି ନଥାନ୍ତ, ତେଣୁ ମୁଁ ତୁମ ସହିତ ଏହି ଚାଲାକି କରିଥିଲି।"

💡 English Meaning: The sage explained the truth: there is no such thing as a magic potion. The gold coins were the profit earned because Rama Natha worked hard farming his land and his wife cleverly sold the bananas. The sage used this trick because Rama Natha was too lazy to listen to logical advice about hard work.

📝 Summary (English Only)

Rama Natha was the son of a wealthy landlord who inherited vast areas of land. However, he was extremely lazy and never worked on his fields. Instead, he was obsessed with a foolish idea that there was a magic potion capable of turning everyday objects into solid gold. He wasted all his time and money searching for this potion, constantly getting cheated by frauds. His wife, Madhumati, was deeply concerned that his careless spending would soon leave them completely broke.

One day, a famous sage named Mahipati arrived in their town. Desperate for wealth, Rama Natha became his follower and begged for the secret of the magic potion. The sage cleverly decided to teach him a lesson. He told Rama Natha that the potion could only be made using five liters of winter morning dew collected entirely by his own hands from banana plants. Motivated by greed, Rama Natha went home, cleared his neglected land, and planted a massive banana plantation. Over the next six years, he worked tirelessly to collect the dew, while his hardworking wife sold the abundant banana crops in the market for a huge profit.

After six long years, Rama Natha finally took the five liters of dew to the sage. The sage performed a mock spell and told him to test it. When the dew failed to turn a copper vessel into gold, Rama Natha was furious and accused the sage of cheating him out of six precious years of his life. At that moment, Madhumati stepped forward with a large box filled with shiny gold coins they had earned from selling the bananas. The sage smiled and explained that there was no magic potion; the real magic was his hard work on the land and his wife's business skills. Rama Natha finally understood this profound wisdom and continued to work hard for the rest of his life.

1. Biography of the Author (କବିଙ୍କ ଜୀବନୀ) ✍️

English Biography: Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (2 October 1869 - 30 January 1948) was India's greatest leader, who employed non-violence to lead the successful campaign for India's independence from British Rule, and in turn inspired movements for civil rights and freedom across the world. 'The Story of My Experiments with Truth', 'Hind Swaraj' or 'Indian Home Rule' are some of the leading books by Mahatma Gandhi. In the present extract, Gandhi focuses on his life and learning at high school.

Odia Biography (ଓଡ଼ିଆରେ): ମୋହନଦାସ କରମଚାନ୍ଦ ଗାନ୍ଧୀ (୨ ଅକ୍ଟୋବର ୧୮୬୯ - ୩୦ ଜାନୁଆରୀ ୧୯୪୮) ଭାରତର ସର୍ବଶ୍ରେଷ୍ଠ ନେତା ଥିଲେ, ଯିଏ ବ୍ରିଟିଶ ଶାସନରୁ ଭାରତକୁ ସ୍ୱାଧୀନ କରିବା ପାଇଁ ଅହିଂସା ମାର୍ଗ ଆପଣାଇଥିଲେ ଏବଂ ସମଗ୍ର ବିଶ୍ୱରେ ନାଗରିକ ଅଧିକାର ଓ ସ୍ୱାଧୀନତା ଆନ୍ଦୋଳନକୁ ପ୍ରେରଣା ଦେଇଥିଲେ। ମହାତ୍ମା ଗାନ୍ଧୀଙ୍କ ଦ୍ୱାରା ରଚିତ କେତେକ ପ୍ରମୁଖ ପୁସ୍ତକ ହେଉଛି 'The Story of My Experiments with Truth', 'Hind Swaraj' ଏବଂ 'Indian Home Rule'। ଏହି ବିଷୟରେ, ଗାନ୍ଧୀ ନିଜର ହାଇସ୍କୁଲ ଜୀବନ ଏବଂ ଶିକ୍ଷା ଉପରେ ଆଲୋକପାତ କରିଛନ୍ତି।

Paragraph 1 Explanation 🎓

Original Text: I was not regarded as a fool at the high school. I always enjoyed the affection of my teachers. Certificates of progress and character used to be sent to the parents every year. I never had a bad certificate. In fact, I even won prizes after I passed out of the second standard. In the fifth and sixth I obtained scholarships of rupees four and ten respectively, an achievement for which I have to thank good luck more than my merit.

English Meaning: Mahatma Gandhi explains that he was never considered a weak or foolish student during his high school days. He was loved and appreciated by all his teachers. His parents always received good reports about his academic progress and personal behavior. After finishing the second standard, he even started winning prizes for his performance. In the fifth and sixth standards, he received scholarships of four and ten rupees, but as a humble person, he believed this success was more because of good luck rather than his actual intelligence.

Paragraph 2 Explanation 🏏

Original Text: My own recollection is that I did not have any high regard for my ability. I used to be astonished whenever I won prizes and scholarships. But I very jealously guarded my character. The least little blemish drew tears from my eyes. When I merited, or seemed to the teacher to merit a rebuke, it was unbearable for me. I remember having once received corporal punishment. I did not so much mind the punishment as the fact that it was considered my desert. I wept piteously. That was when I was in the first or second standard. There was another such incident during the time when I was in the seventh standard. Dorabji Edulji Gimi was the headmaster then. He was popular among the boys, as he was a disciplinarian, a man of method and a good teacher. He made gymnastics and cricket compulsory for the boys of upper standard. I disliked both. I never took part in any exercise, cricket or football, before they were made compulsory. My shyness was one of the reasons for this aloofness, which I now see wrong. I had then the false notion that gymnastics had nothing to do with education. Today I know that physical training should have much place in the curriculum as mental training.

Pronunciation: ମାଇଁ ଓନ୍ ରିକଲେକ୍ସନ୍ ଇଜ୍ ଦ୍ୟାଟ୍ ଆଇ ଡିଡ୍ ନଟ୍ ହ୍ୟାଭ୍ ଏନି ହାଇ ରିଗାର୍ଡ ଫର୍ ମାଇଁ ଏବିଲିଟି... ଟୁଡେ ଆଇ ନୋ ଦ୍ୟାଟ୍ ଫିଜିକାଲ୍ ଟ୍ରେନିଂ ସୁଡ୍ ହ୍ୟାଭ୍ ମଚ୍ ପ୍ଲେସ୍ ଇନ୍ ଦ କରିକୁଲମ୍ ଆଜ୍ ମେଣ୍ଟାଲ୍ ଟ୍ରେନିଂ ।

English Meaning: Gandhi did not consider himself highly talented and was often surprised when he won awards. However, he was highly protective of his moral character. Even a tiny mistake or a scolding from a teacher made him cry bitterly because he could not bear the thought of doing something wrong. He deeply cried when he received physical punishment in junior classes, not because of the physical pain, but because he felt ashamed that he deserved to be punished. Later, in the seventh standard, the popular headmaster, Mr. Gimi, made sports like cricket and gymnastics compulsory. Gandhi disliked sports because he was very shy. Back then, he incorrectly believed that physical games had nothing to do with studying or real education. Today, he realizes that physical fitness is just as important as mental development in a student's life.

Paragraph 3 Explanation 🕰️

Original Text: The reason for my dislike was my keen desire to serve as nurse to my father. As soon as the school closed, I would hurry home and begin serving him. Compulsory exercise came directly in the way of this service. I requested Mr Gimi to exempt me from gymnastics so that I might be free to serve my father. But he would not listen to me. Now it happened that one Saturday, when we had school in the morning, I had to come from home to school for gymnastics at four o'clock in the afternoon. I had no watch, and the cloud deceived me. ଗାନ୍ଧୀ ହସ୍ତାକ୍ଷର ଉପରେ ମଧ୍ୟ କୌଣସି ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱ ଦେଇନଥିଲେ, ଯାହା ପାଇଁ ସେ ପରବର୍ତ୍ତୀ ସମୟରେ ଦକ୍ଷିଣ ଆଫ୍ରିକାରେ ବହୁତ ଅନୁତାପ କରିଥିଲେ। ସେ ଅନୁଭବ କଲେ ଯେ ଖରାପ ହସ୍ତାକ୍ଷର ଏକ ଅସମ୍ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ଶିକ୍ଷାର ପରିଚୟ, ତେଣୁ ପିଲାମାନଙ୍କୁ ଅକ୍ଷର ଶିଖିବା ପୂର୍ବରୁ ପ୍ରଥମେ ଚିତ୍ରାଙ୍କନ ଶିଖିବା ଉଚିତ୍ ଯାହାଦ୍ୱାରା ସେମାନଙ୍କର ହସ୍ତାକ୍ଷର ସୁନ୍ଦର ହେବ। ଅନ୍ୟପକ୍ଷରେ, ସଂସ୍କୃତ ପଢ଼ିବା ତାଙ୍କ ପାଇଁ ବହୁତ କଷ୍ଟକର ଥିଲା କାରଣ ସେଥିରେ ବହୁତ କିଛି ଘୋଷିବାକୁ ପଡୁଥିଲା, ତେଣୁ ସେ ଲୋଭରେ ପଡ଼ି ସହଜ ଲାଗୁଥିବା ପାର୍ସୀ ଶ୍ରେଣୀରେ ବସିବାକୁ ଚେଷ୍ଟା କରିଥିଲେ। କିନ୍ତୁ ତାଙ୍କ ସଂସ୍କୃତ ଶିକ୍ଷକ କୃଷ୍ଣଶଙ୍କର ପାଣ୍ଡ୍ୟାଙ୍କର କୋମଳ ଉପଦେଶ ଓ ଦୟା ତାଙ୍କୁ ଲଜ୍ଜିତ କରିଥିଲା ଏବଂ ସେ ପୁଣି ସଂସ୍କୃତ ଶ୍ରେଣୀକୁ ଫେରିଆସିଥିଲେ। ଏହି ଶିକ୍ଷା ପରବର୍ତ୍ତୀ ଜୀବନରେ ତାଙ୍କୁ ହିନ୍ଦୁ ଧର୍ମଗ୍ରନ୍ଥଗୁଡ଼ିକୁ ପଢ଼ିବା ଏବଂ ବୁଝିବାରେ ବହୁତ ସାହାଯ୍ୟ କରିଥିଲା।

2. Summary (English Only) 🇬🇧

In his autobiography 'My Experiment with Truth', Mahatma Gandhi reflects on his high school experiences, noting that while he did not have a high regard for his own ability, he was never considered a fool. He consistently received good character and progress certificates, enjoyed the affection of his teachers, and even won prizes and monthly scholarships of four and ten rupees. He humbly attributed these achievements to good luck rather than his actual merit. Gandhi was fiercely protective of his character, and any slight blemish or rebuke from a teacher would deeply upset him and bring him to tears.

Gandhi initially harbored a strong dislike for gymnastics and cricket, which his disciplinarian Headmaster, Dorabji Edulji Gimi, had made compulsory for older boys. This aversion was caused by his extreme shyness, a false notion that physical training had no place in proper education, and, most importantly, his deep desire to rush home after school to nurse his sick father. On one occasion, the cloudy weather deceived him because he lacked a watch, causing him to arrive completely late for a Saturday afternoon gymnastics class. He was wrongfully accused of lying about the weather and fined by the headmaster, an incident that caused him severe mental anguish and taught him the valuable life lesson that a man of truth must always be a man of care.

Later in his life, particularly during his time in South Africa, Gandhi deeply regretted neglecting his handwriting during his youth. Having previously believed that good handwriting was unnecessary, he realized that bad penmanship is actually a clear sign of an imperfect education, leading him to advise that young children should be taught drawing first to naturally develop beautifully formed letters. Furthermore, Gandhi struggled greatly with Sanskrit, finding the memorization harder than subjects like geometry, and was briefly tempted to switch to the easier Persian class. However, the gentle kindness and encouragement of his Sanskrit teacher, Krishnashankar Pandya, convinced him to return. Gandhi later felt immense gratitude for this intervention, as the foundational Sanskrit he learned allowed him to take a deep interest in understanding sacred religious books in his adulthood.

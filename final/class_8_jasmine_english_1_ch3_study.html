Wit and Wisdom: Wisdom Paves the Way

Class: VIII

✍️ Writer Biography (ଲେଖକଙ୍କ ଜୀବନୀ)

English:

The play "Wisdom Paves the Way" is an adaptation of a famous traditional Indian folktale. While the original author is unknown, such folktales have been passed down orally from generation to generation. They are designed to teach valuable life lessons. This specific story highlights the power of keen observation, logical deduction, and how true wisdom is far more valuable than just seeing things on the surface.

Odia (ଓଡ଼ିଆରେ):

"ୱିଜଡମ୍ ପେଭସ୍ ଦ ୱେ" (ଜ୍ଞାନ ବାଟ କଢ଼ାଏ) ନାଟକଟି ଏକ ପ୍ରସିଦ୍ଧ ପାରମ୍ପରିକ ଭାରତୀୟ ଲୋକକଥାର ଏକ ରୂପାନ୍ତର ଅଟେ। ଯଦିଓ ଏହାର ମୂଳ ଲେଖକ ଅଜ୍ଞାତ, ଏହିପରି ଲୋକକଥାଗୁଡ଼ିକ ପିଢ଼ି ପରେ ପିଢ଼ି ମୌଖିକ ଭାବରେ ପ୍ରଚଳିତ ହୋଇଆସିଛି। ମୂଲ୍ୟବାନ ଜୀବନଶିକ୍ଷା ଦେବା ପାଇଁ ଏଗୁଡ଼ିକ ଉଦ୍ଦିଷ୍ଟ। ଏହି ନିର୍ଦ୍ଦିଷ୍ଟ କାହାଣୀଟି ତୀକ୍ଷ୍ଣ ପର୍ଯ୍ୟବେକ୍ଷଣ, ତାର୍କିକ ସିଦ୍ଧାନ୍ତ ଏବଂ ପ୍ରକୃତ ଜ୍ଞାନର ଶକ୍ତି ଉପରେ ଆଲୋକପାତ କରେ।

English Meaning:

This section introduces the characters and the setting of the play. The story begins on a hot, dusty road leading to the city of Ujjain. Four young men are walking towards the city. They look tired from a long journey but are very determined to reach their destination.

Word Meanings (ଶବ୍ଦାର୍ଥ) [Easy to Hard]:

  • Dusty (ଡଷ୍ଟି): ଧୂଳିମୟ (Covered with dust)

  • Adorned (ଆଡର୍ଣ୍ଣଡ୍): ସୁସଜ୍ଜିତ (Beautified / decorated)

  • Bustling (ବସଲିଂ): କୋଳାହଳପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ / ବ୍ୟସ୍ତ (Busy / full of activity)

  • Resolute (ରେଜୋଲ୍ୟୁଟ୍): ଦୃଢ଼ସଙ୍କଳ୍ପବଦ୍ଧ (Determined)

  • Tapestries (ଟ୍ୟାପେଷ୍ଟ୍ରିଜ୍): ଚିତ୍ରିତ ମୋଟା ପରଦା (Heavy decorative fabrics)

🔹 Block 2: The Journey and Hopes

Original Text:

RAM DATT: (Wiping his brow) We have journeyed far from home, yet the prospect of finding work still eludes us.

HAR DATT: How long has it been since we left our village?

SHIV DATT: I believe it has been over a week- perhaps even longer.

RAM DATT: Ten days, to be precise. Every day feels longer than the last.

DEV DATT: Ten days are but a short span in the grand scheme of things. We may still have many more roads to wander before we find employment befitting all four of us.

RAM DATT: If only we could secure an audience with the King of Ujjain. I am confident he would recognise our talents and appoint us to his service.

SHIV DATT: I share your belief.

DEV DATT: Indeed, we ought to be united in this hope. That is why we press on towards Ujjain.

HAR DATT: (Pausing thoughtfully) Gaining an audience with the King is no simple task. We need to devise a strategy.

DEV DATT: Let us reach the city gates first. We can deliberate once we are closer.

1. Summary (English)

The play "Wisdom Paves the Way" tells the story of four observant and highly intelligent young men—Ram Datt, Shiv Datt, Har Datt, and Dev Datt—who are journeying to the city of Ujjain in search of employment. They are hopeful that if they can secure an audience with the young King of Ujjain, he will recognize their talents and appoint them to his service. While walking along a dusty road, they notice fresh camel tracks. Through keen observation and logical deduction, they closely scrutinize the surroundings and silently figure out several specific physical conditions of the camel.

Shortly after, a distressed merchant approaches them, anxiously looking for his lost camel. The four men ask the merchant a series of highly specific questions: whether the camel is lame in one leg, blind in its right eye, possesses a short tail, and is suffering from stomach pain. Shocked by their 100% accurate description, the merchant assumes that since they know so much about the animal without claiming to have seen it, they must be the thieves who stole it. Ignoring their calm demeanor, the indignant merchant drags the four men to the King's court to face justice.

In the royal court, the King asks the young men to explain how they knew such intimate details about the missing camel. The men brilliantly break down their deductions: Ram Datt noticed one faint footprint among three clear ones, indicating a limp; Shiv Datt observed that leaves were only eaten on the left side of the road, meaning the camel was blind in its right eye; Har Datt saw blood droplets from mosquito bites, deducing the camel lacked a long tail to swat pests; and Dev Datt noticed faint hind footprints, indicating the camel was walking cautiously to ease belly pain. Original Text:

In response to this invasion, the Government of India sent its troops to defend the people of Kashmir and India's borders on 27 October 1947. A contingent of troops posted to Jammu and Kashmir was airlifted into Srinagar because the land route through Pathankot was not in a good condition.

On 31 October, the D Company of the 4th Battalion of Kumaon Regiment, under the command of Major Somnath Sharma, was also flown into Srinagar. At that time, Major Somnath Sharma's left hand was in plaster, but he insisted on being with his company while in combat. He knew that this mission would be very challenging and told his senior officers, "I know my men better than anyone else, and if they are going into action, they are not going in without me." These words highlighted his commitment to his men and his country.

English Meaning:

To protect Kashmir from the invaders, India sent troops on October 27, 1947, flying them in because the roads were bad. Major Somnath Sharma and his men (D Company) arrived in Srinagar on October 31. Even though Major Sharma's left arm was broken and in a plaster cast, he refused to stay behind. He insisted on going into battle, proving his deep love for his soldiers and his country by saying his men wouldn't fight without him.

🔹 Block 5: The Patrolling and the Surprise Attack

Original Text:

Before their arrival in Srinagar, there had been unfounded rumours of tribal movement towards the village of Badgam, less than 5 kilometres away from the Srinagar airport. The village was on a higher altitude than the airfield, and access was through the village. Hence, it was important for Indian forces to retain control of the area in order to protect troops and facilitate supplies arriving via air.

In reality, the enemy had started arriving in Badgam in small groups to avoid detection. On 03 November 1947, several companies, including Major Somnath Sharma's D Company, were sent to patrol and assess emerging threats in and around the village. Throughout the day, the valley was eerily silent. Around afternoon, the companies were ordered to return one by one. D company with 90 soldiers was the last, and they were expected to return by 2:00 p.m.

When the enemy (tribal guerrilla invaders), under the command of the Pakistani army, saw that only a small group of Indian soldiers were left behind in Badgam, they attacked them. Their initial attack was met with stiff resistance by Major Somnath and his men. However, Major Somnath correctly visualised that the first attack was just a distraction. Shortly thereafter, a major attack came from another side. By then, Major Somnath Sharma and his 90 soldiers were facing about 500 invaders.

ଯୁଦ୍ଧ ଆଗକୁ ବଢ଼ିବା ସହ, ଡି କମ୍ପାନୀ ପାଖରେ ଗୋଳାବାରୁଦ ସରିବାକୁ ଲାଗିଲା। ଯେତେବେଳେ ମେଜର ସୋମନାଥ ଶର୍ମା ଏ ବିଷୟରେ ବ୍ରିଗେଡ୍ ହେଡକ୍ୱାର୍ଟରକୁ ଜଣାଇଲେ, ତାଙ୍କୁ ପଛକୁ ଘୁଞ୍ଚିଆସିବାକୁ ଆଦେଶ ଦିଆଗଲା। ପରିସ୍ଥିତିର ଗମ୍ଭୀରତା ଜାଣି ମଧ୍ୟ ସେ ଏହି ଉତ୍ତର ଦେଇଥିଲେ, "ଶତ୍ରୁମାନେ ଆମଠାରୁ ମାତ୍ର ୫୦ ଗଜ ଦୂରରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି। ସେମାନଙ୍କ ସଂଖ୍ୟା ଆମଠାରୁ ଯଥେଷ୍ଟ ଅଧିକ। ଆମ ଉପରେ ପ୍ରବଳ ଗୁଳିମାଡ଼ ହେଉଛି। କିନ୍ତୁ ଆମେ ଗୋଟିଏ ଇଞ୍ଚ ବି ପଛକୁ ହଟିବୁ ନାହିଁ, ବରଂ ଶେଷ ଲୋକ ଏବଂ ଶେଷ ଗୁଳି ଥିବା ପର୍ଯ୍ୟନ୍ତ ଲଢ଼ିବୁ।"

ନିଜର ମେଜରଙ୍କୁ ହରାଇବା ପରେ ଏବଂ ଶତ୍ରୁଙ୍କ ବିଶାଳ ସଂଖ୍ୟାଗରିଷ୍ଠତା ସତ୍ତ୍ୱେ, ସ୍ୱର୍ଗତ ମେଜର ସୋମନାଥ ଶର୍ମାଙ୍କର ବୀରତ୍ୱପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ କାର୍ଯ୍ୟ ତାଙ୍କ ସୈନିକମାନଙ୍କୁ ଶତ୍ରୁମାନଙ୍କ ଦ୍ୱାରା ପରାସ୍ତ ହେବା ପୂର୍ବରୁ ଆହୁରି ୬ ଘଣ୍ଟା ଧରି ନିଜ ସ୍ଥାନରେ ଲଢ଼ିବାକୁ ପ୍ରେରଣା ଦେଇଥିଲା।

ସେମାନଙ୍କର ଏହି ସାହସିକ ପ୍ରତିରୋଧ ଯୋଗୁଁ ଅତିରିକ୍ତ ସୈନ୍ୟ ଶ୍ରୀନଗରରେ ପହଞ୍ଚିବା ପାଇଁ ସମୟ ମିଳିପାରିଥିଲା, ଯେଉଁଥିରେ ପଞ୍ଜାବ ରେଜିମେଣ୍ଟର ସୈନିକମାନେ ବିମାନ ଯୋଗେ ଆସିଥିଲେ। ମେଜର ସୋମନାଥ ଏବଂ ତାଙ୍କ ସୈନିକମାନଙ୍କ ବୀରତ୍ୱ ଶତ୍ରୁମାନଙ୍କ ମନୋବଳକୁ ସମ୍ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ଭାଙ୍ଗିଦେଇଥିଲା, ଯେଉଁଥିରେ ୩୦୦ ରୁ ଅଧିକ ଶତ୍ରୁ ମୃତାହତ ହୋଇଥିଲେ ଏବଂ ସେମାନଙ୍କ ନେତା ମଧ୍ୟ ଆହତ ହୋଇଥିଲେ।

ଏହିପରି ଅନେକ ଦିଗରୁ, ଡି କମ୍ପାନୀର ବଳିଦାନ ଏବଂ ମେଜର ସୋମନାଥଙ୍କ ନେତୃତ୍ୱ ଭାରତ ପାଇଁ ଶ୍ରୀନଗରକୁ ରକ୍ଷା କରିପାରିଥିଲା। ୪ କୁମାଉଁ ବାଟାଲିୟନକୁ 'ବ୍ୟାଟଲ୍ ଅନର୍, ଶ୍ରୀନଗର' ସମ୍ମାନ ପ୍ରଦାନ କରାଯାଇଥିଲା। ମେଜର ସୋମନାଥ ମାତ୍ର ୨୪ ବର୍ଷ ବୟସରେ ଦେଶ ପାଇଁ ନିଜ ଜୀବନ ବଳିଦାନ ଦେଇଥିଲେ। ଏହି ଉଲ୍ଲେଖନୀୟ ବୀରତ୍ୱ ପାଇଁ ମେଜର ସୋମନାଥ ଶର୍ମାଙ୍କୁ ପରମବୀର ଚକ୍ର (PVC) ପ୍ରଦାନ କରାଯାଇଥିଲା। ତାଙ୍କୁ ଦିଆଯାଇଥିବା ପ୍ରଶଂସାପତ୍ର ତାଙ୍କର ବୀରତ୍ୱ, ଦେଶପ୍ରେମ ଏବଂ ସାହସକୁ ପ୍ରତିଫଳିତ କରେ। ସେ ଥିଲେ ପି.ଭି.ସି. ର ପ୍ରଥମ ପ୍ରାପ୍ତକର୍ତ୍ତା, ଯାହା ତାଙ୍କର ଅଦମ୍ୟ ସାହସ ଏବଂ ସର୍ବୋଚ୍ଚ ବଳିଦାନ ପାଇଁ ତାଙ୍କୁ ମରଣୋତ୍ତର ଭାବେ ପ୍ରଦାନ କରାଯାଇଥିଲା।

English Meaning:

When Major Sharma's men ran out of bullets, his bosses told him to retreat. But he famously radioed back: "We are outnumbered and under heavy fire. But I will not retreat an inch. We will fight to the last man and the last bullet." Moments later, while helping a soldier load a gun, an enemy bomb hit him, and he died instantly. Even after his death, his men were so inspired by his courage that they kept fighting for 6 more hours. This delay gave the Indian Army enough time to fly in more soldiers and save the airport. The enemy lost 300 men and gave up. For saving Srinagar at the young age of 24, Major Somnath Sharma was awarded India's highest bravery award, the Param Vir Chakra, becoming its very first recipient.

1. Summary (English)

The chapter "A Tale of Valour: Major Somnath Sharma and the Battle of Badgam" recounts the extraordinary heroism and supreme sacrifice of Major Somnath Sharma, the first recipient of India's highest military decoration, the Param Vir Chakra. The story begins in August 1947, when Pakistan launched "Operation Gulmarg" to capture the Kashmir Valley, violating an existing peace agreement with Maharaja Hari Singh. The Pakistani army trained and armed about 1000 tribal invaders to enter Kashmir, overthrow the government, and seize strategic locations like the Srinagar airport. In response to this massive invasion, the Government of India airlifted troops to defend Srinagar on October 27, 1947.

On October 31, Major Somnath Sharma arrived in Srinagar with the D Company of the 4th Battalion of the Kumaon Regiment. Even though his left hand was fractured and in a plaster cast, he insisted on leading his men into combat, demonstrating his profound commitment to his soldiers and his country. On November 3, while D Company was patrolling the village of Badgam, they were suddenly ambushed by a massive force of about 500 Pakistani-backed invaders. Major Sharma quickly realized that this was a major attack aimed at capturing the Srinagar airfield. He bravely requested air strikes and laid out ground panels under heavy fire to guide the Indian aircraft toward the enemy targets.

Facing a devastating seven-to-one disadvantage, Major Sharma refused the order to pull back.

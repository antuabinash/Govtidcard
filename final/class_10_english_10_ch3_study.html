The Solitary Reaper – Study Material Class 10 ENGLISH

📖 About the Poet: William Wordsworth

1. Original Text 

William Wordsworth was born on 7th April 1770, in Cockermouth in the Lake District, England. He is regarded as a worshipper of nature. Love of nature is a major theme of his poetry. He wrote about ordinary men and women in the language of the ordinary people. For him poetry is "the spontaneous overflow of powerful feelings" arising from "emotions recollected in tranquility." He died at Rydal Mount and Gardens, United Kingdom on April 23, 1850.

2. Word Meanings (English → Odia)

  • Worshipper: ଉପାସକ / ପୂଜାରୀ

  • Nature: ପ୍ରକୃତି

  • Theme: ବିଷୟବସ୍ତୁ

  • Ordinary: ସାଧାରଣ

  • Spontaneous: ସ୍ୱତଃସ୍ଫୁର୍ତ୍ତ

  • Overflow: ଉଦ୍‌ବେଳନ / ଉଛୁଳିବା

  • Emotions: ଆବେଗ / ଭାବନା

  • Recollected: ସ୍ମରଣ କରାଯାଇଥିବା / ଏକତ୍ରିତ

  • Tranquility: ଶାନ୍ତି / ନୀରବତା

3. Odia Meaning (Translation / Explanation) ଉଇଲିୟମ୍ ୱାର୍ଡସୱର୍ଥ ୭ ଏପ୍ରିଲ୍ ୧୭୭୦ ରେ ଇଂଲଣ୍ଡର ଲେକ୍ ଡିଷ୍ଟ୍ରିକ୍ଟ ଅନ୍ତର୍ଗତ କକରମାଉଥ୍ ଠାରେ ଜନ୍ମଗ୍ରହଣ କରିଥିଲେ। ତାଙ୍କୁ ପ୍ରକୃତିର ଜଣେ ମହାନ୍ ଉପାସକ ଭାବରେ ବିବେଚନା କରାଯାଏ। ପ୍ରକୃତି ପ୍ରେମ ତାଙ୍କ କବିତାର ଏକ ପ୍ରମୁଖ ବିଷୟବସ୍ତୁ ଅଟେ। ସେ ସାଧାରଣ ଲୋକମାନେ କହୁଥିବା ଭାଷାରେ ସାଧାରଣ ନରନାରୀଙ୍କ ବିଷୟରେ କବିତା ଲେଖିଥିଲେ। ତାଙ୍କ ମତରେ କବିତା ହେଉଛି "ଶାନ୍ତ ପରିବେଶରେ ସ୍ମରଣ କରାଯାଇଥିବା ଆବେଗରୁ ଜାତ ଶକ୍ତିଶାଳୀ ଅନୁଭୂତିର ସ୍ୱତଃସ୍ଫୁର୍ତ୍ତ ଉଦ୍‌ବେଳନ"। ୨୩ ଏପ୍ରିଲ୍ ୧୮୫୦ ରେ ଯୁକ୍ତରାଜ୍ୟର ରାଇଡାଲ୍ ମାଉଣ୍ଟ୍ ଆଣ୍ଡ୍ ଗାର୍ଡେନ୍ସଠାରେ ତାଙ୍କର ଦେହାନ୍ତ ହୋଇଥିଲା।

🎵 Stanza 1: The Reaper in the Field

1. Original Text & Pronunciation

Behold her single in the field,

 Yon solitary Highland Lass!

Reaping and singing by herself;

Stop here, or gently pass!

Alone she cuts and binds the grain,

 And sings a melancholy strain;

O listen! for the Vale profound

Is overflowing with the sound.

English-Odia Pronunciation:

ବିହୋଲ୍ଡ ହାର୍ ସିଙ୍ଗଲ୍ ଇନ୍ ଦ ଫିଲ୍ଡ,

ୟନ୍ ସଲିଟାରୀ ହାଇଲ୍ୟାଣ୍ଡ ଲାସ୍!

ରିପିଙ୍ଗ୍ ଆଣ୍ଡ୍ ସିଙ୍ଗିଙ୍ଗ୍ ବାଇ ହର୍‌ସେଲ୍ଫ;

ଷ୍ଟପ୍ ହିଅର୍, ଅର୍ ଜେଣ୍ଟଲି ପାସ୍!

ଏଲୋନ୍ ସି କଟସ୍ ଆଣ୍ଡ ବାଇଣ୍ଡସ୍ ଦ ଗ୍ରେନ୍,

ଆଣ୍ଡ ସିଙ୍ଗସ୍ ଏ ମେଲାଙ୍କୋଲି ଷ୍ଟ୍ରେନ୍;

ଓ ଲିସିନ୍! ଫର୍ ଦ ଭେଲ୍ ପ୍ରୋଫାଉଣ୍ଡ

ଇଜ୍ ଓଭରଫ୍ଲୋଇଙ୍ଗ୍ ୱିଥ୍ ଦ ସାଉଣ୍ଡ।

2. Word Meanings (English → Odia)

  • Behold: ଦେଖ (To look at or see)

  • Single / Solitary: ଏକାକୀ / ନିର୍ଜନ (Single or alone)

  • Yon: ସେହି / ଦୂରରେ ଥିବା (Over there)

  • Highland Lass: ପାର୍ବତ୍ୟ ଅଞ୍ଚଳର ବାଳିକା (A girl who lives in the mountain regions of Scotland)

  • Reaping: ଶସ୍ୟ କାଟୁଛି

    inds: ବାନ୍ଧୁଛି

    Melancholy strain: ଦୁଃଖଦାୟକ ଗୀତ / ବିଷାଦ ସଙ୍ଗୀତ (Sad song)

  • Vale profound: ଗଭୀର ଉପତ୍ୟକା (Deep valley)

3. Odia Meaning (Translation / Explanation)

କବି କହୁଛନ୍ତି, ସେହି ପାର୍ବତ୍ୟ ଅଞ୍ଚଳର ଝିଅଟିକୁ ଦେଖ ଯିଏ ଏକାକୀ ବିଲରେ ଶସ୍ୟ କାଟୁଛି ଏବଂ ଗୀତ ଗାଉଛି। କବି ପାନ୍ଥମାନଙ୍କୁ ସେଠାରେ ଅଟକିବାକୁ କିମ୍ବା ତାକୁ ବ୍ୟାଘାତ ନକରି ଧୀରେ ଚାଲିଯିବାକୁ କହୁଛନ୍ତି। ସେ ଏକୁଟିଆ ଶସ୍ୟ କାଟୁଛି ଓ ବାନ୍ଧୁଛି ଏବଂ ଏକ ଦୁଃଖଭରା ଗୀତ ଗାଉଛି। ଶୁଣ! ତା'ର ସେହି ଦୁଃଖଭରା ମଧୁର ସ୍ୱରରେ ସମ୍ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ଗଭୀର ଉପତ୍ୟକାଟି ପ୍ରତିଧ୍ୱନିତ ହେଉଛି।

4. English Meaning (Simple Explanation) The poet asks the readers or passersby to look at the young Scottish girl working all alone in the field. She is cutting the grain, binding it, and singing a sad song by herself. He advises people to either stop and listen to her or pass by quietly without disturbing her. Her beautiful but sad voice completely fills the deep valley.

🐦 Stanza 2: Comparison with the Birds

1. Original Text & Pronunciation

No Nightingale did ever chaunt

 More welcome notes to weary bands

 Of travellers in some shady haunt,

Among Arabian sands:

 A voice so thrilling ne'er was heard

In spring-time from the Cuckoo-bird,

Breaking the silence of the seas

Among the farthest Hebrides.

English-Odia Pronunciation:

ନୋ ନାଇଟିଙ୍ଗେଲ୍ ଡିଡ୍ ଏଭର୍ ଚାଣ୍ଟ

ମୋର୍ ୱେଲକମ୍ ନୋଟ୍ସ ଟୁ ୱିଅରି ବ୍ୟାଣ୍ଡସ୍

ଅଫ୍ ଟ୍ରାଭେଲର୍ସ ଇନ୍ ସମ୍ ସେଡି ହଣ୍ଟ,

ଆମଙ୍ଗ ଆରବିଆନ୍ ସ୍ୟାଣ୍ଡସ୍:

ଏ ଭଏସ୍ ସୋ ଥ୍ରିଲିଂ ନେଭର୍ ୱାଜ୍ ହର୍ଡ

ଇନ୍ ସ୍ପ୍ରିଙ୍ଗ୍-ଟାଇମ୍ ଫ୍ରମ୍ ଦ କୁକୁ-ବାର୍ଡ,

ବ୍ରେକିଙ୍ଗ୍ ଦ ସାଇଲେନ୍ସ ଅଫ୍ ଦ ସିଜ୍

ଆମଙ୍ଗ ଦ ଫାର୍ଦେଷ୍ଟ ହେବ୍ରିଡିସ୍।

 

 

2. Word Meanings (English → Odia)

  • Chaunt: ଗାଇବା (Chant, sing)

  • Weary bands: କ୍ଳାନ୍ତ ଯାତ୍ରୀ ଦଳ (Tired group of people)

  • Shady haunt: ମରୂଦ୍ୟାନ / ବିଶ୍ରାମ ସ୍ଥାନ (Oasis)

  • Arabian sands: ଆରବର ମରୁଭୂମି (Desert of Arabia)

  • Thrilling: ରୋମାଞ୍ଚକର

  • Farthest Hebrides: ସ୍କଟଲ୍ୟାଣ୍ଡର ଦୂରବର୍ତ୍ତୀ ଦ୍ୱୀପପୁଞ୍ଜ (Remote islands of Scotland)

3. Odia Meaning (Translation / Explanation)

ଆରବ ମରୁଭୂମିର ମରୂଦ୍ୟାନରେ ବିଶ୍ରାମ ନେଉଥିବା କ୍ଳାନ୍ତ ଯାତ୍ରୀମାନଙ୍କୁ ଆନନ୍ଦ ଦେବା ପାଇଁ ନାଇଟିଙ୍ଗେଲ୍ ପକ୍ଷୀ ଯେପରି ଗୀତ ଗାଏ, ଏହି ଝିଅଟିର ସ୍ୱର ତା'ଠାରୁ ମଧ୍ୟ ଅଧିକ ମଧୁର ଏବଂ ସ୍ୱାଗତଯୋଗ୍ୟ ଥିଲା। ବସନ୍ତ ଋତୁରେ ସ୍କଟଲ୍ୟାଣ୍ଡର ଦୂରବର୍ତ୍ତୀ ହେବ୍ରିଡିସ୍ ଦ୍ୱୀପପୁଞ୍ଜର ନୀରବତାକୁ ଭାଙ୍ଗୁଥିବା କୋଇଲିର ରୋମାଞ୍ଚକର ସ୍ୱରଠାରୁ ମଧ୍ୟ ଏହି ଝିଅର ସ୍ୱର ବହୁତ ମଧୁର ଥିଲା।

 

4. English Meaning (Simple Explanation) The poet compares the girl's singing to two beautiful singing birds. He says her voice is sweeter and more welcoming than a Nightingale singing to tired travelers resting in an Arabian desert oasis. Her voice is also more thrilling and exciting than the song of a Cuckoo-bird in springtime, which breaks the silence of the remote Hebrides islands.

🤔 Stanza 3: Guessing the Theme

1. Original Text & Pronunciation

Will no one tell me what she sings?-

 Perhaps the plaintive numbers flow

For old, unhappy, far-off things,

And battles long ago:

Or is it some more humble lay,

 Familiar matter of to-day?

 Some natural sorrow, loss, or pain,

That has been, and may be again?

English-Odia Pronunciation:

ଉଇଲ୍ ନୋ ୱାନ୍ ଟେଲ୍ ମି ହ୍ୱାଟ୍ ସି ସିଙ୍ଗସ୍?-

ପରହାପ୍ସ ଦ ପ୍ଲେଣ୍ଟିଭ୍ ନମ୍ବର୍ସ ଫ୍ଲୋ

ଫର୍ ଓଲ୍ଡ, ଅନ୍‌ହ୍ୟାପି, ଫାର୍-ଅଫ୍ ଥିଙ୍ଗସ୍,

ଆଣ୍ଡ ବ୍ୟାଟେଲସ୍ ଲଙ୍ଗ୍ ଏଗୋ:

ଅର୍ ଇଜ୍ ଇଟ୍ ସମ୍ ମୋର୍ ହମ୍ବଲ୍ ଲେ,

ଫ୍ୟାମିଲିଆର୍ ମ୍ୟାଟର୍ ଅଫ୍ ଟୁ-ଡେ?

ସମ୍ ନାଚୁରାଲ୍ ସରୋ, ଲସ୍, ଅର୍ ପେନ୍,

ଦ୍ୟାଟ୍ ହ୍ୟାଜ୍ ବିନ୍, ଆଣ୍ଡ ମେ ବି ଏଗେନ୍?

2. Word Meanings (English → Odia)

  • Plaintive numbers: ଦୁଃଖଭରା ସଙ୍ଗୀତ (Sad music)

  • Far-off things: ବହୁ ପୁରାତନ କଥା (Things of the distant past)

    Battles: ଯୁଦ୍ଧ

  • Humble lay: ସାଧାରଣ ଗୀତ (Ordinary song)

  • Familiar matter: ପରିଚିତ ଘଟଣା ବା ଦୈନନ୍ଦିନ ଘଟଣା

  • Sorrow / Loss / Pain: ଦୁଃଖ / କ୍ଷତି / କଷ୍ଟ

3. Odia Meaning (Translation / Explanation) କବି ଝିଅଟି ଗାଉଥିବା ଭାଷା ବୁଝିପାରୁନାହାଁନ୍ତି, ତେଣୁ ସେ ପଚାରୁଛନ୍ତି ଯେ ତାଙ୍କୁ କେହି କହିବେ କି ସେ କି ଗୀତ ଗାଉଛି? କବି ଅନୁମାନ କରୁଛନ୍ତି ଯେ, ବୋଧହୁଏ ତାର ଏହି ଦୁଃଖଦ ଗୀତଟି କୌଣସି ପୁରୁଣା ଦୁଃଖଦ ଘଟଣା ବା ଅତୀତର ଯୁଦ୍ଧ ବିଷୟରେ ହୋଇପାରେ। କିମ୍ବା ଏହା ଆଜିକାଲିର ଦୈନନ୍ଦିନ ଜୀବନର କୌଣସି ସାଧାରଣ ଘଟଣା, ଦୁଃଖ, କ୍ଷତି ବା କଷ୍ଟ ବିଷୟରେ ହୋଇପାରେ ଯାହା ପୂର୍ବରୁ ଘଟିଛି ଏବଂ ପୁଣି ଘଟିପାରେ।

4. English Meaning (Simple Explanation) Because the poet does not understand the girl's language, he wonders what the song is about. He makes some guesses: maybe she is singing a sad song about unhappy historical events or ancient battles. Or, perhaps she is singing an ordinary song about an everyday occurrence, such as natural human sorrow, a personal loss, or pain that humans experience repeatedly.

❤️ Stanza 4: An Everlasting Memory

1. Original Text & Pronunciation

Whate'er the theme, the Maiden sang

As if her song could have no ending;

I saw her singing at her work,

 And o'er the sickle bending;-

 I listen'd, motionless and still;

And, as I mounted up the hill,

The music in my heart I bore,

Long after it was heard no more.

English-Odia Pronunciation:

ହ୍ୱାଟ୍ଏଭର୍ ଦ ଥିମ୍, ଦ ମେଡେନ୍ ସାଙ୍ଗ

ଆଜ୍ ଇଫ୍ ହାର୍ ସଙ୍ଗ କୁଡ୍ ହ୍ୟାଭ ନୋ ଏଣ୍ଡିଙ୍ଗ୍;

ଆଇ ସ ଅ ହାର୍ ସିଙ୍ଗିଙ୍ଗ୍ ଆଟ୍ ହାର୍ ୱାର୍କ,

ଆଣ୍ଡ ଓଭର୍ ଦ ସିକିଲ୍ ବେଣ୍ଡିଙ୍ଗ୍;-

ଆଇ ଲିସିନ୍‌ଡ, ମୋସନଲେସ୍ ଆଣ୍ଡ ଷ୍ଟିଲ୍;

ଆଣ୍ଡ, ଆଜ୍ ଆଇ ମାଉଣ୍ଟେଡ୍ ଅପ୍ ଦ ହିଲ୍,

ଦ ମ୍ୟୁଜିକ୍ ଇନ୍ ମାଇ ହାର୍ଟ ଆଇ ବୋର୍,

ଲଙ୍ଗ୍ ଆଫ୍ଟର୍ ଇଟ୍ ୱାଜ୍ ହର୍ଡ ନୋ ମୋର୍।

2. Word Meanings (English → Odia)

  • Theme: ବିଷୟବସ୍ତୁ

  • Maiden: କୁମାରୀ ଝିଅ

  • Sickle: ଦାଆ (A tool for cutting grass or paddy)

  • Motionless and still: ସ୍ଥିର ଏବଂ ନିଶ୍ଚଳ (Without movement)

  • Mounted: ଉପରକୁ ଚଢିଲେ

  • Bore: ବହନ କଲେ (Carried)

📝 5. Detailed English Summary

William Wordsworth’s poem "The Solitary Reaper" depicts a profound experience he had with a 'Solitary Highland Lass' in Scotland. The poet is walking through a valley when he spots a young girl reaping grain all alone in a field. She is singing a sad, deeply emotional song that echoes throughout the entire deep valley. The poet is so moved by her voice that he asks anyone passing by to either stop and listen quietly or leave gently without disturbing her work and her song.

The poet finds her voice extraordinarily beautiful. He compares her singing to the songs of two famous birds known for their melodies. He states that her voice is more comforting than a Nightingale singing to tired travelers in the hot Arabian deserts, and more thrilling than the Cuckoo-bird breaking the silence of the remote Hebrides islands in spring. Because the girl is singing in a Scottish dialect, the poet cannot understand the lyrics of her song. He imagines the song's theme, guessing it might be about ancient battles, past tragedies, or even everyday human sorrows and pain.

Ultimately, the exact meaning of the song does not matter to the poet. He is captivated by the raw emotion and beauty of her endless singing as she bends over her sickle. He stands completely motionless and spellbound. When he finally resumes his journey up the hill, he can no longer hear her voice, but the mesmerizing music remains safely stored in his heart as a beautiful, everlasting memory.

1. Original Book Text The North-East India is a collective name for the eastern-most parts of India representing both a geographic and administrative division of the country. The region comprises eight states, namely Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Sikkim and Tripura. The contiguous seven states Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Nagaland are commonly described as the "Land of Seven Sisters" because of their interdependence on each other. English Meaning (Elaborate in Simple Language) The term "North-East India" is used to describe the most eastern parts of the country, acting as both a geographical and administrative section of India. This region is made up of eight different states: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Sikkim, and Tripura. Seven of these states share connected borders and rely on each other so much that they are popularly called the "Land of Seven Sisters". Sikkim, however, does not share a direct border with these seven states because a narrow strip of land called the Siligudi corridor separates it. Because of this physical separation, Sikkim is not counted as one of the Seven Sisters. Instead, it is affectionately considered the "little brother" to these seven amazing states.

🌾 Paragraph 3: The Festivals of Assam (Bihu)

1. Original Text   Blessed with lush greenery and the mighty River Brahmaputra, the people of Assam have a lot to celebrate. Bihus are among the major cultural festivals of Assam. They are a series of three prominent festivals- Rongali Bihu, Kongali Bihu and Bhogali Bihu. Rongali Bihu also known as Bohag Bihu is celebrated in mid-April with the onset of spring and the beginning of the sowing season It also marks the start of a New Year there. Kongali or Kaati Bihu is celebrated in mid-October when the fields are lush but the barns are empty. On this occasion, people light lamps and pray to God for a thriving harvest season and to guide souls to heaven. Bhogali, called Magh Bihu is observed in mid-January. The festival symbolises the end of the harvest season. It is a thanksgiving when the crops have been harvested and the barns are full.

📝 1. Complete Summary (English)

🌍 Introduction to the Beautiful North-East The North-East region of India is a collective geographical and administrative division consisting of eight distinct states. Seven of these states—Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Nagaland—are deeply interconnected and popularly celebrated as the "Land of Seven Sisters". Sikkim, which is geographically separated by the Siligudi corridor, lovingly plays the role of a little brother to these seven states. This entire region is a vibrant tapestry of diverse cultures, where native communities and tribes celebrate unique, agriculture-based festivals that are always completed with soulful traditional music and rhythmic dance.

🌾 The Vibrant Festivals of Assam and Arunachal Pradesh In the lush, green state of Assam, the most significant cultural celebrations are the three Bihu festivals. Rongali (or Bohag) Bihu warmly welcomes the New Year and the spring sowing season in mid-April. Later in mid-October, when barns are empty, Kongali Bihu is celebrated by lighting lamps and praying for a thriving harvest and heavenly guidance for departed souls. Finally, Bhogali Bihu in mid-January is a massive thanksgiving feast marking the end of the harvest. Bihu dances are joyous, performed in traditional attire with instruments like the dhol and buffalo-horn pepa. Up in the mountainous Arunachal Pradesh, the Losar festival brings in the New Year with families cleaning their homes. The Galo tribe worships goddess Mopin for wealth during their harvest festival, featuring the Popir dance, while the Ziro festival gathers music lovers globally every September.

🤝 Joyous Celebrations in Manipur, Mizoram, and Meghalaya Manipur showcases a beautiful family bonding festival called Ningol Chakouba, where married daughters and their children are treated to a sumptuous feast and gifts. Their New Year, Cheiraoba, happens in April and involves a unique ritual of climbing the nearest hilltops to symbolize rising to a higher plane in life. In Mizoram, the Spring Festival called Chapchar Kut is celebrated by men and women of all ages performing folk dances to the beats of drums and gongs in traditional headgear. Meanwhile, in Meghalaya, the Garos celebrate their major harvest festival, Wangala, in November. Also known as the Hundred-drum Festival, it features men and women dancing rhythmically in parallel lines while a hundred drums are beaten to mark the end of hard agricultural labor.

🏹 Traditions of Tripura, Nagaland, and Sikkim Tripura draws thousands of people in July for Karachi Puja, a week-long festival involving animal sacrifice and the worship of fourteen deities at a special temple in old Agartala. Nagaland honors its rich history through festivals like Moastu, celebrated right after the sowing season with colorful clothes and wild boar tusk decorations. Their most spectacular event is the Hornbill Festival in December, where 16 tribes showcase their hunting and warring skills over ten days. Over in the Himalayan state of Sikkim, festivals follow the Buddhist calendar. They celebrate Saga Dawa by visiting monasteries, Losoong as a vibrant harvest festival featuring masked Chaam dances, Losar as the Tibetan New Year, and Tihaar as their brilliant festival of lights.

✨ Conclusion Ultimately, hardly a single month passes in the North-East without a festival or two. While most of these celebrations hold deep religious significance—thanking gods for a good harvest or asking for protection from calamities—they also serve a greater purpose. They beautifully encourage artistic activities and bring out the natural, vibrant talent of the region's people, keeping their oral history and traditions alive for generations.

🌾 ଆସାମ ଏବଂ ଅରୁଣାଚଳ ପ୍ରଦେଶର ଉତ୍ସବ ସବୁଜିମାରେ ଭରା ଆସାମର ସବୁଠାରୁ ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ସାଂସ୍କୃତିକ ଉତ୍ସବ ହେଉଛି ବିହୁ, ଯାହା ତିନୋଟି ପର୍ଯ୍ୟାୟରେ ପାଳିତ ହୁଏ। ମଧ୍ୟ-ଏପ୍ରିଲରେ ରୋଙ୍ଗାଲି ବିହୁ ନୂତନ ବର୍ଷ ଏବଂ ବୁଣାବୁଣି ଋତୁର ଆରମ୍ଭକୁ ସ୍ୱାଗତ କରେ। ଯେତେବେଳେ କ୍ଷେତ ସବୁଜ ଥାଏ କିନ୍ତୁ ଶସ୍ୟାଗାର ଖାଲି ଥାଏ, ସେତେବେଳେ ମଧ୍ୟ-ଅକ୍ଟୋବରରେ କୋଙ୍ଗାଲି ବିହୁ ପାଳନ କରି ଲୋକେ ଭଲ ଅମଳ ପାଇଁ ଦୀପ ଜାଳି ପ୍ରାର୍ଥନା କରନ୍ତି। ଶେଷରେ, ମଧ୍ୟ-ଜାନୁଆରୀରେ ଭୋଗାଲି ବିହୁ ସମୟରେ ଅମଳ ଶେଷ ହେବା ଖୁସିରେ ଈଶ୍ୱରଙ୍କୁ ଧନ୍ୟବାଦ ଜଣାଇ ଭୋଜିର ଆୟୋଜନ କରାଯାଏ। ପାରମ୍ପରିକ ପୋଷାକ, ଢୋଲ ଏବଂ ମଇଁଷି ଶିଙ୍ଗରୁ ତିଆରି ପେପା ବିନା ବିହୁ ନୃତ୍ୟ ସମ୍ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ହୁଏନାହିଁ। ପାର୍ବତ୍ୟ ରାଜ୍ୟ ଅରୁଣାଚଳ ପ୍ରଦେଶରେ ନୂଆବର୍ଷ ଲୋସାର୍ ପର୍ବରେ ଲୋକେ ଘର ସଫା କରନ୍ତି। ଗାଲୋ ଜନଜାତିର ଲୋକେ ଅମଳ ପର୍ବ ମୋପିନ୍ ସମୟରେ ଧନ ପାଇଁ ଦେବୀଙ୍କୁ ପୂଜା କରନ୍ତି ଏବଂ ପୋପିର୍ ନୃତ୍ୟ କରନ୍ତି, ଯେତେବେଳେ କି ପ୍ରସିଦ୍ଧ ଜିରୋ ସଙ୍ଗୀତ ମହୋତ୍ସବ ପ୍ରତିବର୍ଷ ସେପ୍ଟେମ୍ବରରେ ଆୟୋଜିତ ହୁଏ।WithTeachers.in

🤝 ମଣିପୁର, ମିଜୋରାମ ଏବଂ ମେଘାଳୟର ଆନନ୍ଦ ଉଲ୍ଲାସ ମଣିପୁରରେ ପରିବାରକୁ ଏକାଠି କରିବାର ଏକ ସୁନ୍ଦର ପର୍ବ ନିଙ୍ଗୋଲ୍ ଚାକୌବା ପାଳନ କରାଯାଏ, ଯେଉଁଠାରେ ବିବାହିତା ଝିଅମାନଙ୍କୁ ଭୋଜି ଏବଂ ଉପହାର ଦିଆଯାଏ। ସେମାନଙ୍କ ନୂଆବର୍ଷ ଚେଇରାଓବା ଏପ୍ରିଲରେ ପାଳିତ ହୁଏ, ଯେଉଁଥିରେ ଲୋକେ ଜୀବନରେ ଉନ୍ନତି କରିବା ଆଶାରେ ପାହାଡ଼ ଉପରକୁ ଚଢ଼ନ୍ତି। ମିଜୋରାମରେ, ଚାପଚାର୍ କୁଟ୍ ନାମକ ବସନ୍ତ ଉତ୍ସବରେ ସବୁ ବୟସର ଲୋକେ ପାରମ୍ପରିକ ପୋଷାକ ପିନ୍ଧି ଢୋଲ ତାଳରେ ଲୋକନୃତ୍ୟ କରନ୍ତି। ଅନ୍ୟପକ୍ଷରେ, ମେଘାଳୟରେ ଗାରୋ ଜନଜାତି ନଭେମ୍ବର ମାସରେ ସେମାନଙ୍କର ପ୍ରମୁଖ ଅମଳ ପର୍ବ ୱାଙ୍ଗାଲା ପାଳନ କରନ୍ତି। ଏହାକୁ ଶହେ ଢୋଲର ପର୍ବ ମଧ୍ୟ କୁହାଯାଏ, ଯେଉଁଥିରେ କଠିନ ପରିଶ୍ରମର ଅନ୍ତକୁ ଚିହ୍ନିତ କରିବା ପାଇଁ ଶହେଟି ଢୋଲ ବଜାଇବା ସହ ସ୍ତ୍ରୀ ଓ ପୁରୁଷମାନେ ଧାଡ଼ି ବାନ୍ଧି ତାଳବଦ୍ଧ ନୃତ୍ୟ କରନ୍ତି।

🏹 ତ୍ରିପୁରା, ନାଗାଲାଣ୍ଡ ଏବଂ ସିକିମର ପରମ୍ପରା ତ୍ରିପୁରାରେ ଜୁଲାଇ ମାସରେ ଏକ ସପ୍ତାହ ଧରି କରାଚି ପୂଜା ପାଳନ କରାଯାଏ, ଯେଉଁଥିରେ ଆଗରତାଲାର ଏକ ମନ୍ଦିରରେ ଚଉଦ ଜଣ ଦେବତାଙ୍କୁ ପୂଜା କରାଯିବା ସହ ପଶୁବଳି ଦିଆଯାଏ ଏବଂ ମେଳା ଲାଗେ। ନାଗାଲାଣ୍ଡ ନିଜର ଇତିହାସକୁ ଉତ୍ସବ ମାଧ୍ୟମରେ ବଞ୍ଚାଇ ରଖିଛି; ସେଠାରେ ବିହନ ବୁଣାବୁଣି ପରେ ରଙ୍ଗବେରଙ୍ଗର ପୋଷାକରେ ମୋଆଷ୍ଟୁ ପର୍ବ ପାଳନ କରାଯାଏ। ନାଗାଲାଣ୍ଡର ସବୁଠାରୁ ଆକର୍ଷଣୀୟ ପର୍ବ ହେଉଛି ଡିସେମ୍ବରରେ ହେଉଥିବା ହର୍ଣ୍ଣବିଲ୍ ଫେଷ୍ଟିଭାଲ୍, ଯେଉଁଠାରେ ୧୦ ଦିନ ଧରି ୧୬ ଟି ଜନଜାତି ସେମାନଙ୍କର ଯୋଦ୍ଧା କୌଶଳ ପ୍ରଦର୍ଶନ କରନ୍ତି। ହିମାଳୟ କୋଳରେ ଥିବା ସିକିମରେ ବୌଦ୍ଧ କ୍ୟାଲେଣ୍ଡର ଅନୁଯାୟୀ ଉତ୍ସବ ପାଳନ କରାଯାଏ। ସେଠାରେ ସାଗା ଦାୱା ସମୟରେ ବୌଦ୍ଧ ମଠ ପରିଦର୍ଶନ, ମୁଖା ପିନ୍ଧି ଚାମ୍ ନୃତ୍ୟ ସହ ଲୋସୁଙ୍ଗ୍ ନାମକ ଅମଳ ପର୍ବ, ତିବ୍ଦତୀୟ ନୂଆବର୍ଷ ଲୋସାର୍ ଏବଂ ଦୀପାବଳି ପରି ଆଲୋକର ପର୍ବ ତିହାର୍ ମହାସମାରୋହରେ ପାଳିତ ହୁଏ।

✨ ଉପସଂହାର ସାମଗ୍ରିକ ଭାବରେ ଦେଖିଲେ, ଉତ୍ତର-ପୂର୍ବ ଭାରତରେ ଏପରି କୌଣସି ମାସ ନାହିଁ ଯେଉଁଥିରେ ଉତ୍ସବର ଆୟୋଜନ ହୁଏନାହିଁ। ଏହି ଉତ୍ସବଗୁଡ଼ିକ ଭଲ ଫସଲ ପାଇଁ କିମ୍ବା ବିପଦରୁ ରକ୍ଷା ପାଇବା ପାଇଁ ଈଶ୍ୱରଙ୍କୁ ଧନ୍ୟବାଦ ଦେବାର ଧାର୍ମିକ ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱ ବହନ କରେ। ଏହା ବ୍ୟତୀତ, ଏହି ପର୍ବଗୁଡିକ ସେଠାକାର ଲୋକଙ୍କ ଭିତରେ ଥିବା ପ୍ରାକୃତିକ କଳା କୌଶଳକୁ ପ୍ରଦର୍ଶନ କରିବା ସହ ସେମାନଙ୍କର ସାଂସ୍କୃତିକ ପରମ୍ପରାକୁ ଉତ୍ସାହିତ କରିଥାଏ।

D. Let's understand the text: (ପାଠ୍ୟଟିକୁ ବୁଝିବା)

1. What is North-East India? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ଉତ୍ତର-ପୂର୍ବ ଭାରତ କ'ଣ?)

 Ans: North-East India is a collective name for the eastern-most parts of India, representing both a geographic and administrative division of the country.


2. Which states form the North-East India? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: କେଉଁ ରାଜ୍ୟଗୁଡିକ ଉତ୍ତର-ପୂର୍ବ ଭାରତ ଗଠନ କରନ୍ତି?)

 Ans: The region comprises eight states: Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Sikkim and Tripura


3. Which states are called the "Land of Seven Sisters"? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: କେଉଁ ରାଜ୍ୟଗୁଡିକୁ "ସପ୍ତଭଗିନୀଙ୍କ ଦେଶ" କୁହାଯାଏ?)

 Ans: The contiguous seven states of Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Nagaland are described as the "Land of Seven Sisters". (Note: The text lists six but calls them "seven states"; Tripura is the seventh).


4. Why are they called so? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ସେମାନଙ୍କୁ କାହିଁକି ଏପରି କୁହାଯାଏ?)

 Ans: They are called so because of their interdependence on each other.


5. Why is Sikkim not included among the "Seven Sisters"? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ସିକ୍କିମ କାହିଁକି "ସପ୍ତଭଗିନୀ" ଙ୍କ ମଧ୍ୟରେ ଅନ୍ତର୍ଭୁକ୍ତ ନୁହେଁ?)

 Ans: Sikkim is not included because it is geographically not a contiguous part of the northeast region and is separated from it by the Siligudi corridor.


6. What role does it play? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ଏହା କି ଭୂମିକା ଗ୍ରହଣ କରେ?)

 Ans: Sikkim has happily taken up the role of being a "little brother" to the amazing seven sisters.


7. What is culture? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ସଂସ୍କୃତି କ’ଣ?)

 Ans: According to the glossary, culture is "the habits, traditions and beliefs of a country".


8. People here celebrate their festivals with... (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ଏଠାକାର ଲୋକମାନେ ସେମାନଙ୍କର ପର୍ବ ପାଳନ କରନ୍ତି...) 

Ans: ...great enthusiasm and joy.


9. Many of their festivals are based on... (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ସେମାନଙ୍କର ଅନେକ ପର୍ବ ... ଉପରେ ଆଧାରିତ)

Ans: ...agriculture.


10. What are the two important parts of celebrating their festivals? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ସେମାନଙ୍କର ପର୍ବ ପାଳନ କରିବାର ଦୁଇଟି ଗୁରୁତ୍ୱପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ ଅଂଶ କ’ଣ?)

 Ans: Bihus are among the major cultural festivals of Assam.

Ans: Assam: Assam celebrates three major festivals known as the Bihus. Rongali Bihu in April marks the New Year and sowing season with joyous dance. Kongali Bihu in October is a more somber time when people light lamps and pray for a good harvest. Finally, Bhogali Bihu in January is a thanksgiving festival celebrating the end of the harvest when barns are full.

Arunachal Pradesh: The people of this picturesque state celebrate several unique festivals. Losar is the New Year festival, where people clean their homes to welcome the new beginning. Mopin, the harvest festival of the Galo tribe, involves worshipping a goddess for prosperity and performing the Popir folk dance. Arunachal Pradesh also hosts the famous Ziro festival, an iconic outdoor music event.

Manipur: Manipur's festivals focus on strengthening social and spiritual bonds. Ningol Chakouba is a charming festival that renews the relationship between married daughters and their parents with a grand feast. Cheiraoba, the state's New Year festival in April, is marked by cleaning homes and a ritual of climbing the nearest hilltops, which is believed to help one rise to a higher plane in life.

Mizoram: In Mizoram, the most important occasion is the Chapchar Kut, celebrated as the Spring Festival. During this vibrant celebration, men and women of all ages wear their beautiful traditional dresses and headgear. They perform traditional folk dances and sing songs, with the festive air enhanced by the sound of drums, gongs, and cymbals.

Meghalaya: Meghalaya is home to the Garos, who celebrate the Wangala, or the Hundred-drum Festival. This is a major harvest festival celebrated in November to mark the end of a period of hard work. The celebration features the rhythmic beating of 100 drums, and a unique dance performed by men and women in two parallel lines.

Tripura: Tripura celebrates the unique Karachi Puja, a week-long festival in July held at old Agartala. The festival involves animal sacrifice and the worship of fourteen different gods as instructed by Lord Shiva. Thousands of people throng the state during this time to enjoy the many cultural programmes and fairs.

Nagaland: Nagaland's festivals are the soul of its culture and help preserve its oral history. The Moastu festival is celebrated after the sowing season, with people dancing in colourful clothes. The most famous is the Hornbill Festival, a 10-day event in December that showcases the cultural heritage of 16 different tribes through dance and sporting events.

Sikkim: Sikkim's festivals are celebrated with great pomp as per the Buddhist calendar. Saga Dawa commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and death of Lord Buddha. Losoong is the harvest festival celebrated with Chaam dances in colourful masks and archery contests. They also celebrate Losar (Tibetan New Year) and Tihaar, a festival of lights.

1. Describe the three different Bihu festivals of Assam, noting their time of celebration and purpose. (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ଆସାମର ତିନୋଟି ଭିନ୍ନ ଭିନ୍ନ ବିହୁ ପର୍ବ, ସେଗୁଡ଼ିକର ପାଳନ ସମୟ ଏବଂ ଉଦ୍ଦେଶ୍ୟ ଉଲ୍ଲେଖ କରି ବର୍ଣ୍ଣନା କର ।) 


Ans: The three prominent Bihu festivals are Rongali, Kongali, and Bhogali.

1.      Rongali Bihu, or Bohag Bihu, is celebrated in mid-April to mark the onset of spring, the beginning of the sowing season, and the New Year.

2.      Kongali Bihu, or Kaati Bihu, is celebrated in mid-October when barns are empty. On this occasion, people light lamps and pray for a thriving harvest.

3.      Bhogali Bihu, or Magh Bihu, is observed in mid-January. It is a thanksgiving festival that marks the end of the harvest season when the barns are full.

2. Describe the celebration of the Wangala festival (The Hundred-drum Festival) in Meghalaya. What does the dance look like? (ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଅର୍ଥ: ମେଘାଳୟର ୱାଙ୍ଗାଲା ପର୍ବ (ଶହେ-ଡ୍ରମ୍ ପର୍ବ)ର ପାଳନ ବିଷୟରେ ବର୍ଣ୍ଣନା କରନ୍ତୁ । ଏହି ନୃତ୍ୟ କିପରି ଦେଖାଯାଏ?)


 Ans: Wangala is the most important harvest festival of the Garos in Meghalaya, celebrated in November to mark the end of hard work. It is called the Hundred-drum Festival because 100 drums are beaten together during the celebration, accompanied by the blowing of horns. The dance performed is unique and disciplined. It features two parallel lines, one of men and the other of women, clad in their festive costumes. While the men beat the drums, the two lines move forward together in a rhythmic manner.

Ans: Both Losar and Cheiraoba are important New Year festivals, but they have distinct rituals.

Celebrations are more physical and festive, involving the Chaam dance performed in brightly coloured masks, along with archery contests and feasting.
